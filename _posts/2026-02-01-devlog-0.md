# Devlog 0: Setting the Foundation

## Introduction
I am starting this devlog to document my personal projects and technical learning outside of university. 

Mainly, I'm starting this series to hold myself accountable. Every week, I will post a new devlog, describing the key takeaways from what I have been building, the concepts I have learned, and the roadblocks I've faced along the way.

## Recent Milestone: Options 101
I recently completed the **Akuna Capital Options 101** course. While my background is in CS and AI, I have always had an interest in the financial markets. However, I had no knowledge of how the markets work at all.

This course was really helpful, teaching me all about a wide range of concepts involved in trading and the markets. Not only did Options 101 teach me formulas and core concepts like **The Greeks** and the different types of contracts which can be traded, but it taught me everything from the basics, including participants like **Market Makers** and their roles, definitions of basic terminology, and different **Order Types**.

I found the lessons which started from the absolute basics extremely helpful, and cleared up a lot of confusion which I held about ideas which I had heard of in the past.

## Current Project: Adaptive Kalman Filter
While im learning C++ and before my C++ skills are strong enough to develop my Parser, I'm going to be improving my **Kalman Filter**. I made this project at the start of January, and managed to achieve a Sharpe Ratio of 1.67 over a 10 year backtest. Some combinations of Q and R offered higher Sharpe Ratios, but I was afraid of overfitting to the history of the relationship between the pair of tickers that I chose, and so I settled with values which gave me this score.

I am going to spend the next week or two improving my Kalman Filter. The next feature I aim to implement will be **Adaptive Measurement Noise**. The Sharpe Ratio I achieved was a result of a **Grid Search**, however, the data being searched covered 10 years of market data. The implementation of this feature will allow me to optimise the **Process Noise R**, accounting for data over a recent period, making it more accurate. I am also programming a **Transaction Cost Engine**, which will account for **Spread**,  **Slippage**, **Commissions** and **Fees** as to better simulate a real market environment, and improving my program to not make a trade if the costs resulting from these frictions against the trade which would be faced when live trading.

As I'm writing these devlogs, I will be documenting important reflections I've made during this process.
